The HR file problem

Every new hire generates the same small storm of paper. Forms go out by email; some come back signed, some unsigned, and one never comes back at all. The offer letter sits in one inbox, the benefits form in a shared drive, and the acknowledgment as a scan on someone's desktop.

Nobody notices the gaps while things are calm. They surface later — when payroll asks a question, when a policy needs proof of acknowledgment, when the file is requested. Then the packet is reconstructed from memory and mailboxes, and "complete" turns out to have been an assumption, not a fact.

What changes on Outcome Cloud™

On Outcome Cloud™, the packet is defined once as a checklist: the documents a role requires, which of them need a signature, and what counts as received. From there, each item is tracked to a state — submitted, read, signed, resolved, recorded — not to a folder.

Intake happens through a portal, not an inbox. IDP Outcomes™ reads what is submitted and checks it against the checklist. Anything missing or unsigned becomes a resolution item a person can act on, not a gap discovered months later. When every item checks out, the packet is recorded as one employee file — the outcome: what got done with it.

The packet, checked item by item

Stage 1

Assemble

Read & Check

MaxxOwl™ SI reads each submitted document: what it is, whose it is, and whether the required fields are actually there.

Sign

Resolve

Stage 5

Record

The documents HR runs on

The same checking applies to every document an employee file accumulates over a career:

Offer letters

Issued, signed, and filed with the terms as accepted — the first item in the packet and the first one checked.

Tax & eligibility forms

The standard new-hire tax and work-eligibility forms, collected, checked for completeness, and filed with the packet. Which forms your organisation must collect is set by your own policies and advisers.

Policy acknowledgments

Handbooks and policies are only acknowledged when the signed acknowledgment is in the file — the signature is an item, not an assumption.

Benefits enrollment

Elections and beneficiary forms tracked to completion, so enrollment ends with finished files, not a list of who still owes a form.

Reviews & evaluations

Completed reviews and signed evaluations added to the same record, in order, retrievable when the next cycle starts. Direct deposit authorisations, certifications, and role-change forms join the same file as they occur.

What changes for the HR team

The chase disappears. Instead of asking around for a missing form, HR opens a queue that already knows which packets are complete, which items are outstanding, and how long they have waited. Follow-up becomes a reminder the system sends, not a hallway conversation.

And when the file is asked for — by the employee, payroll, or an auditor — it is produced as one record: what was collected, what was signed, and what had to be resolved along the way. Complete stops being a hope and becomes a status you can read.

Frequently asked questions

What makes an HR file "complete"?

Every item on the packet checklist for that employee's role is present, legible, and signed where a signature is required. Completeness is a checked status, item by item — not a folder someone believes is finished.

How are signatures collected?

Inside the packet flow: items needing a signature are routed for e-signature or digital signature, and the signed version is checked back into the packet. The dedicated signing resource is SecureSignNow.

What happens when a document is missing or wrong?

It becomes a resolution item in Outcome Workspace™ — named, reasoned, and owned — instead of a silent gap. The employee is reminded, the item arrives or is corrected, and it is checked again. The file stays visibly incomplete until then.

Can we retrieve one employee's complete file later?

Yes — recorded in Outcome Repository™ as one record per employee, including what was collected, what was signed, and what needed resolution before the file closed.

Does this replace our HR team or our other systems?

No. It handles the documents: the collecting, reading, checking, chasing, and filing. People still handle the people — the conversation, the judgement, the decision. The platform makes sure the paperwork those decisions rest on is actually complete.
